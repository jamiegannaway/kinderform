# Why foster carers stop, and what helps them stay

> **Status: first draft.** Desk review, 26 November 2026. It was made with an AI-assisted search of official statistics, peer-reviewed papers and the web. See [how we made this](https://www.kinderform.org/about#how). On 26 November 2026 we matched the main Ofsted figures for 2025–26 to Ofsted's own page, by computer. No person has checked any figure yet. Everything we propose is for discussion. It is not yet part of the Kinderform standard, the open standard that the project behind kinderform.org is drafting. The [research log](../research-log.json) says what each review could mean for the project.

Grades:

- **A:** randomised or strong quasi-experimental
- **B:** controlled comparison, large observational study, or robust official statistics
- **C:** descriptive, survey or qualitative
- **D:** anecdote, a single site, guidance, or read secondhand

## In short

1. **About 1 in 8 approved mainstream fostering households stops each year in England.** In 2025–26, 4,440 of 37,890 households de-registered: 12% (grade B).
2. **Much of that leaving is a normal part of life.** Carers aged 60 or over were 35% of carers who de-registered. Among carers under 50 who de-registered, 19% did so to adopt the child. So "carers who stop" does not mean "carers the service failed".
3. **Carers stay when the service supports them reliably.** The same things come up in England, the US, Australia and Sweden:
   - a good relationship with the service
   - being treated as an equal member of the team
   - dependable breaks and help out of hours
   - fair handling of allegations
   - clear, adequate money

   All of them are things services can change (grade C).
4. **Early leaving is the clearest link to the first step.** In 2020–21, 30% of households that de-registered did so within two years of approval (grade B).
5. **Mockingbird is the best-evidenced way to keep carers, but it was not tested in a trial.** Households in it had much lower odds of de-registering than similar households (grade B).
6. **Training alone has weak evidence.** A trial of a training course in Wales found no difference on its main measure at 12 months (grade A).
7. **No study links a good assessment to a longer fostering career.** We treat that link as an idea to test, not a finding.

## 1. How many carers stop, in the official figures

What the words mean:

- **Mainstream fostering** is every foster home except kinship care, where a child lives with family or friends.
- **De-registration** is a household leaving a service's register. It includes households that move to another service.
- From 2024–25, Ofsted splits those who leave into "leaver", "transfer" and "other".
- Ofsted rounds figures to the nearest 5. Services take part by choice: 475 of 482 did in 2025–26.
- Ofsted added new reasons for leaving in 2020–21, so reasons are hard to compare across years.
- The reasons are what services record, not what carers say.

**What this means for Kinderform:** the official reasons say what happened, such as retirement, a move to another service or adoption. They do not say why. "Personal circumstances" and "ceasing to foster" cover half of all leaving and could hide reasons the service could fix. Section 5 suggests a way to find out.

## 2. Why carers stay or stop

| Finding | Source | Grade | What carries over to Kinderform |
|---|---|---|---|
| The relationship with the service and professional support matter most to carers staying. | Gouveia and others, 2021 (a review of 49 studies); Hanlon and others, 2021 | B and C | High. Services control these. Both are already in the library. |
| Carers' plans to keep fostering went with how they rated support, not with who they were. | 60% of current carers had thought about stopping. Training and expectations happen before approval. |
| Satisfaction with the recruitment worker went with plans to keep fostering. | Engelhardt and others, 2025 | C | High. The most direct link between the first step and staying. Already in the library. |
| About 85% of carers kept fostering after an allegation that was not proven. 40% were not offered independent support. | Plumridge and Sebba, Rees Centre, University of Oxford, 2016 (England; 190 carers) | C | High. How allegations are handled is a service process. |
| 66% of carers who faced an allegation thought about stopping during the investigation. 57% got no independent support. | The Fostering Network, 2021 survey, in evidence to the Education Select Committee | C | High. Self-reported, by a charity. |

**Reading across the table:** the reasons are much the same in England, the US, Australia and Sweden. Almost all are about how the service and the system behave, not about who the carer is. That supports Kinderform's rule of assessing services, not people. The weakness is design: most of this research asks carers what they intend, at one point in time. It does not follow who actually leaves.

## 5. What it could mean for a Kinderform standard beyond the enquiry

All of this is for discussion. Each idea is about what a service measures about itself. None of it scores, ranks or predicts any carer or applicant.

1. **The same promise for every contact.** "A quick, kind and considered reply" should also cover questions during the assessment, the first match, the first hard night and any allegation. Basis: the recruitment worker study, the "single trusted relationship" and the early leaving figures.
2. **A record of the service's own contacts in the first year.** The service logs its planned contacts with each new household and whether they happened. It also logs who was named for help out of hours, and whether a break was given when asked for. It reports only totals, such as "the share of planned first-year contacts that happened". It never flags a carer.
3. **Short, anonymous questions to carers about the service.** Three to five questions at set points: after approval, after the first match, and at 6 and 12 months. For example:
   - "I was told what to expect."
   - "I felt treated as an equal member of the team."
   - "I knew who to call out of hours."
   - "I understood how my allowance works."

   Use wording close to *State of the Nations*, so results compare with national figures. Report only for groups of at least 10 people.
4. **Exit conversations that assess the service, not the carer.** Offer every household that stops an optional conversation, ideally with someone outside their own team. Sort the reasons into two groups:
   - life reasons, such as retirement, health, adopting the child or moving house;
   - service reasons, such as support, communication, respect, breaks, clarity about money, allegations or information for matching.

   Map them to Ofsted's own reasons, then add the service reasons underneath "ceasing to foster" and "personal circumstances".
5. **What services could measure about their own support:**
   - time to the first visit from the supervising social worker after approval
   - changes of supervising social worker in the first year
   - the share of carers with someone approved for overnight care
   - time to respond when a carer asks for help
   - the share of allegations where independent support was offered on day one
   - whether allowances were paid on time
   - households that de-register within two years of approval, as a share of approvals, in totals only
6. **Carry the honest preview forward.** At approval and at the first match, go back to what the person was told at enquiry. Say what we said, what it is actually like, and what we will do.
7. **Peer contact from approval.** Offer every newly approved household an introduction to an experienced carer or a support group within the first month. It costs little. Its effect alone is not proven.

**What Kinderform will not do:** some services and suppliers build "risk of leaving" models from case records. Kinderform will not. They label people, and they may come true by themselves, if services invest less in "likely leavers". They also use sensitive data. The evidence says the reasons lie in how services behave, so measuring the service is fairer and more useful.

## 6. Cautions

- **Do not overclaim Mockingbird.** Say "lower odds" and "not randomised". Do not repeat that its later evaluation was stopped, as we could not confirm it.
- **Charity surveys are also campaigns.** *State of the Nations* is the best UK source of carers' views. But carers chose to take part, and it comes with policy asks. Present it as what the carers who responded said.
- **Do not blame supervising social workers.** "Lack of support" usually reflects caseloads, turnover and how the system is designed. Measure at team and service level, in totals.
- **Allegations are about keeping children safe.** Work on allegations must be about fairness, communication and support alongside a thorough investigation. It must never mean less scrutiny. Children's safety comes first.
- **Not all leaving is bad.** Adopting the child, special guardianship and retirement are good or natural endings. Treating all leaving as failure would push services to discourage good endings.
- **Comparing councils and independent agencies is sensitive.** Independent agencies lose fewer households, but they also take carers who moved from councils. Do not make league tables.
- **Words.** Say "carers who stop fostering", not market words. Some sources use them, and we do not repeat them.
- **Data protection.** Exit conversations and feedback hold personal and sometimes allegation-related details. Collect only what is needed, report in totals, and keep free text out of carers' records.

## 7. Gaps and limits

- **No study links the assessment experience to how long people foster.** This is the biggest gap for Kinderform, and a natural question for a pilot that services agree to.
- **Ofsted's reasons are recorded by services and are broad.** We did not take the full 2025–26 reasons, the split between councils and independent agencies, or time since approval from Ofsted's data tables.
- **Wales and Northern Ireland have no comparable yearly figures.** The Scottish figures come from a search result.
- **Mockingbird's later evaluation:** we could not confirm its status, and found no results.
- **Not reviewed, or read only as abstracts:**
  - Hebert and Kulkin
  - Rhodes, Orme and others, beyond the library entry
  - McDermid and others, 2012
  - the KEEP report
  - Fosterline and peer mentoring
- **Most research measures what carers intend, not who leaves.** It is mostly at one point in time, and current carers are those who stayed.
- **Children's outcomes,** such as how stable placements are, appear in some evaluations. Kinderform works on the adult side only, so we leave them out.
