# Automate the admin, keep the conversation human

> **Status: first draft.** Quick scan and a design principle, 25 November 2026. Jamie Gannaway accepted the principle; its wording is a draft for discussion with fostering services. The evidence was found with an AI-assisted web search and read from summaries, press releases and abstracts. None of it is about fostering enquiries, and **none has been checked by a person** against the full source. Check the source before you quote anything, starting with the list under "Check before public use". The [research log](../research-log.json) says what each review could mean for the project.

Grades: A = randomised or strong quasi-experimental; B = controlled comparison or robust official statistics; C = descriptive, survey or qualitative; D = anecdote, single site, self-reported or secondhand.

## The principle

Recruitment teams are small and stretched. Kinderform should help by letting software do the routine admin around an enquiry, so that staff time goes on people.

| | What it covers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Automate** | Routine steps that follow a rule and involve no judgement about a person | The acknowledgement within 5 minutes. Sending the enquiry to the right local or kinship team. Booking a call and sending one reminder. "What happens next and how long it takes" messages. Keeping the record. Producing the figures for the Ofsted return. A flag to staff when someone has waited too long. |
| **Assist, and a person approves** | Drafting | A reply drafted with AI, approved by a member of staff before it is sent. A summary of a call, checked by the person who made the call. |
| **Never automate** | Anything that is a conversation or a judgement | The first conversation. Any view on whether a person is suitable. Anything that scores, ranks or predicts a person. Ending contact with someone. |

Four rules go with it:

1. **Say when a message is automated, and always offer a person.** Every automated message gives a team and a phone number.
2. **Nobody has to talk to a chatbot to make an enquiry.** A person can always ask for a human.
3. **Automation never closes an enquiry.** Only the person who enquired, or a member of staff, can.
4. **Measure the right things.** Count the time staff get back and how often people have to chase for news. Do not count "enquiries handled without a person" as a success.

## What we chose not to say

The first wording was "automate as much of the intake as possible". We did not adopt it:

- **It would harm trust.** It reads as replacing the people who do this work, and as treating people who offer to care for a child as cases to process.
- **The evidence points to human contact as the thing that matters.**
- **Automated front doors can add work.**

## The evidence

### People who enquire value a person

- In fostering and adoption research, the first call and the service's communication are where people are most often put off (Wilson and others, 2005; Chanmugam and others, 2016; Keogh and Svensson, 1999). See the engagement review. Grade C.
- People who dropped out of a remote sign-up process asked for more human contact (Haun and others, 2023). Grade C.
- A Government Digital Service blog (2016) on DVLA's contact centre reported that most callers wanted a phone number and a person to talk to. Grade D.
- A 2025 study in China found people saw more risk in a government chatbot than in a human agent and were less willing to use it (Song, Natori and Yu). Grade C; a different country and service.

### Taking admin away from staff can give time back

- Council pilots of AI note-taking in adult social care report large savings. Somerset Council reported a 46% cut in weekly admin time. Swindon reported a 63% cut in time spent on assessments and case notes, in a pilot with 19 social workers from April to July 2024. These are councils' own figures for one supplier's product. Grade D.
- An Alan Turing Institute evaluation of the same kind of tool, with 91 staff in 3 English councils, found staff reported saving time and being more present in conversations, with some concerns about accuracy (reported by Community Care). Grade C.
- The Ada Lovelace Institute's study "Scribe and prejudice?" described AI note-taking in social care as showing promise for admin tasks. Read from a news summary only. Grade C.
- *Does it carry over?* Partly. This is note-taking after a conversation in adult social care, not fostering recruitment. It supports the "assist, and a person checks" group, with checks for accuracy.

### Telling people where things stand reduces chasing

- The Government Digital Service wrote in 2015 that DVLA received 2.4 million calls a year from people only checking on an application. Automated status messages were built to cut this. Grade D; old.
- The Cabinet Office said in 2019 that it expected GOV.UK Notify to save about £35 million a year. That is a forecast, not a measured result. Grade D.
- *Does it carry over?* Yes, as an idea. It matches "people chasing for news" in the engagement review.

### Digital front doors can create more work

- A modelling study using published evidence estimated that putting online, phone or video contact first at GP surgeries would probably **raise** GP workload: by 25% for online, 3% for phone and 31% for video in its main case (Salisbury, Murphy and Duncan, 2020). Grade B/C; a model, and GP care is not fostering.
- *Lesson:* an automated step only saves staff time if it settles something. If it just adds another way in, people use both.

### Automated judgements about people have caused serious harm

- Australia's Robodebt scheme and the Dutch childcare benefits scandal are the best-known cases. Amnesty International's 2021 report on the Dutch case found racial profiling built into the system that flagged claims as possibly fraudulent. Grade C.
- *Lesson:* this is why "never automate" covers any view on whether a person is suitable, and why nothing may score, rank or predict a person.

## What this means in practice

- The example forms and schemas should make the "automate" steps easy.
- The enquiry record could say whether the acknowledgement was automated and whether the first contact was made by a person. A check could then stop a first conversation being recorded as automated. This is not in the schema yet.
- Public wording: say "taking admin away from stretched teams". Do not say "efficiency" or "throughput".

## Check before public use

1. Somerset 46% and Swindon 63%: council press releases about one supplier. Do not quote as independent evidence.
2. Alan Turing Institute evaluation: read the report itself, not the news summary.
3. Ada Lovelace Institute, "Scribe and prejudice?": confirm the date, method and findings.
4. Salisbury and others (2020): confirm the 25%, 3% and 31% figures and what they assume.
5. DVLA 2.4 million calls (2015): old; look for a current figure.
6. GOV.UK Notify £35 million a year: a forecast from 2019.
