# Evidence update: November 2026

This update explains what we added to the research pages in November 2026, and what it means. In short, fast replies still matter, but speed alone is not enough. People are now lost when contact lapses or passes between organisations. We also corrected an often-quoted figure on withdrawals. Most of the new review is not yet checked by a person.

## What the new evidence means

1. **Speed alone is not enough.** An evaluation of Foster with North East, a regional hub where councils recruit foster carers together, found that initial visits got much faster. But compared with similar areas, there was no measurable gain in applications or approvals. (Corrected 14 November 2026: in 2023–24 the measured effects were negative and statistically significant, meaning unlikely to be down to chance. In 2024–25 none were significant. Checked against the report.) Our case for fast replies still stands, because the biggest early loss is people who are never reached. But we should not claim that speed on its own leads to more approvals.
2. **People are lost now when contact lapses or passes between organisations.** The most clearly documented weak point is the hand-off, when a hub passes a person on to a council. The Department for Education now requires a named "journey guide" in end-to-end hubs. These are hubs that handle the whole journey, from enquiry to approval.
3. **Of all the findings on what makes people act, named and personal messages are the most likely to carry over.** The evidence comes from a jobcentre trial, not from fostering, so it needs testing in fostering and adoption.
4. **Correction: the 61% withdrawal figure is a share of completed applications.** The often-quoted 61% is 61% of *completed* applications (3,290 of 5,370 in 2024–25), not of all applications received. We checked this against Ofsted's main findings.
5. **Our proposals for the standard are for discussion only.** The review proposes a revised standard for first contact. It also proposes a new `Interaction` record, with one row for each contact between a service and a person. This would sit alongside the Enquiry record, our record of the enquiry itself. Nothing in our data standard (the `schema/` folder) has changed.

## What we added in November

**A new review: [intake interactions](../reports/2026-11-intake-interactions.md).** It covers everything that happens after someone gets in touch about fostering or adoption, up to a formal application. That includes:

- acknowledgements and first calls
- follow-ups, information events and home visits
- contact with experienced carers and adopters
- handling people who say "not now"
- hand-offs between organisations
- digital and artificial intelligence (AI) tools
- conversations when someone withdraws

We made the review with an AI-assisted research tool. We checked a few citations by hand on 14 November 2026. The rest are not yet checked by a person against the originals.

**How enquiry forms differ (added 15 November 2026).** The research page now compares 17 enquiry forms without naming the services. They come from our census of enquiry forms, which records what each form asks. We show each measure on its own, so no one can trace a form from one chart to the next. The shortest form asks 5 questions and the longest asks 17. Every independent agency form asks about a spare room, and no council or hub form does. Only 4 of the 16 forms we checked say when you will hear back. A computer program that reads public web pages took these figures from the live pages on 14 November 2026. They are not yet checked by a person. Each service will see its own entries privately before we publish anything by name. See [how enquiry forms differ](https://www.kinderform.org/research#how-forms-differ).

**Library entries.** We added 7 entries to the [evidence library](../library.md), in a new section on what happens after someone gets in touch. We added 3 more to the section on who thinks about fostering and what stops them. They are Rodger et al. 2006, Randle et al. 2014 and Sebba 2012. We added 4 official sources to the list of official figures and policy. We also rewrote the list of gaps we know about.
