# UK fostering and adoption intake forms: what they ask and what guidance surrounds them

**OUCM census v0.1. Checked November 2026.**

---

Public-sector primary fostering enquiry forms across the UK still ask for very little: name, contact details, postcode and how you heard. The risky material sits elsewhere. Consents and marketing are bundled into submission, only a minority give a concrete response time, and the real screening questions are moved into "initial referral", "initial visit" and "application" forms that are almost never published.

**How to read this report.** Every dataset row records whether the form was observed directly, seen through a search-index snapshot, or described secondhand (procedure manuals, statements of purpose, privacy notices, council papers). No forms were submitted. No staff are named. Question labels are paraphrased. Children's data and decisions about children are out of scope.

**Interpretation used.** A *primary* form is the first-contact public form (enquire, register interest, information pack, call back, chat). A *secondary* form is any form after first contact and before Form F assessment, published or only described: initial referral or screening, initial visit, application to foster, registration or expression of interest, and consent to checks. *Guidance* means helper text, eligibility statements, reasons for sensitive questions, privacy and marketing handling, response times, "what happens next", allowances, accessibility, non-digital routes and save-and-return. This first version treated adoption as a comparison. The dataset now covers fostering and adoption equally.

## Summary

- **Primary forms are short and similar; secondary forms are where the questions are.** Public primary fostering forms mostly ask for name, phone, email, postcode, source, type of fostering and a free-text box. The sector's procedure manuals and hub process pages show the topics that matter for assessment moving to an unpublished "initial enquiry/referral form" and an "application form" completed after a call or home visit. These topics include household numbers, a separate bedroom, working hours, motivation, health, childcare experience, education, employment, addresses and relationships.
- **Guidance is uneven, and the gaps are consistent.** Only a minority of public front doors publish a concrete first-contact time. These range from one working day to two working days. One directly observed public form signs submitters up to a newsletter automatically and takes consent to pass details to a council "for checks" at first contact. Others use separate, required consents for contact and processing, with a separate newsletter sign-up. Public pages give conflicting phone hours, membership counts and labels, for example an enquiry form described as an "online application form".
- **Independent agency forms ask earlier and more.** Every independent agency form recorded asks about a spare room, and most make nearly every field required. The largest agency group's shared template also asks about own children, professional experience with children, previous fostering and previous adoption, and asks for citizenship or immigration status. Another, on a form it calls a "preliminary application", asks about criminal convictions, number of spare bedrooms, a driving licence and occupation. In the English regulations these are Stage 1 topics, which begin only once a person applies.

## Key findings

1. **There is a shared national definition of an enquiry, but no shared form.** Ofsted's annual fostering data collection counts an initial enquiry only if it is "actionable": it gives names and contact details so the service can respond. Ofsted counts it per household, not per contact, so several contacts from one person count as one enquiry. Ofsted added this clarification in its guidance on fostering dataset changes for 2023–24 onwards. The DfE's February 2026 plan still notes variations in how fostering services define enquiries. So OUCM should adopt Ofsted's definition rather than invent one. A first-contact record is an Ofsted enquiry once it holds enough to respond.
2. **The scale of drop-off is official and recent.** Ofsted's official statistics, *Fostering in England 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025* (published 26 November 2025), recorded 149,795 initial enquiries in England in 2024–25, a 14.3% rise from 131,055 the year before. Applications received fell to 8,290, from 8,485. Of the 5,370 applications completed in-year, 2,050 were approved, 3,290 were withdrawn (2,175 by the applicant, 1,115 by the service) and 30 were rejected. The withdrawal share of completed applications is therefore 61% (OUCM calculation from Ofsted figures). The DfE's *Renewing Fostering* plan (February 2026) says interest in fostering is not the problem. It cites almost 150,000 enquiries but only 7,365 newly approved foster carers, and 6% of enquiries going on to application. The same plan says people who express an interest wait weeks or months for a reply or face delays in assessment, and that within local authorities 59% of fostering assessments take over six months and 29% take over eight months.
3. **England now has 14 DfE-funded hubs, and they are changing role.** The DfE announced on 8 July 2026 that four new hubs (Black Country, North London, South East London, South West London) would join the ten existing fostering recruitment hubs and go live before the end of the year, bringing the national total to 14. The DfE wants all hubs to become "end-to-end", taking on assessment, approval and support, not just the front door. The DfE's March 2026 programme booklet expects national milestone data on enquiries, expressions of interest, initial visits, application starts, panels and approvals. That is effectively a secondary-stage vocabulary OUCM should align with.
4. **Old forms remain live beside new front doors.** The six North London boroughs moved to Foster North London from 1 October 2026. Two legacy consortium enquiry pages for the same boroughs were still indexed, with different required fields, and the one re-read on 14 November gave no notice of the move. A council-consortium form for the North West still routes Manchester enquiries by council-tax authority, although Manchester belongs to Foster for Greater Manchester.
5. **Wales publishes its enquiry-stage data scope more openly than any other nation.** Several Foster Wales council privacy notices state what is collected at enquiry. Newport, Caerphilly and Rhondda Cynon Taf list name, date of birth, address, contact details, employment, family and relationships, and previous partners. Torfaen also lists gender, plus the name, date of birth and gender of all other residents. They also state that enquiry data are not shared with other organisations at enquiry stage. Rhondda Cynon Taf states a 12-month retention after a closed enquiry. This is more than the visible web forms ask, which suggests the extra items are collected on the first call.

## Stage analysis against the official framework

**England, fostering.** Regulation 26 of the Fostering Services (England) Regulations 2011 (as substituted on 1 July 2013) applies only where a person applies to become a foster parent and the fostering service decides to assess. Stage 1 then requires the Schedule 3 Part 1 information for the applicant and household. That includes full name, address and date of birth; health supported by a medical report; other adults and children in the household; accommodation; outcomes of previous fostering or adoption applications; two referees; an enhanced DBS certificate for the applicant and every adult household member; and current and previous relationships. A provider may reject at any point, but no more than 10 working days after Stage 1 information is complete. Stage 2 (Schedule 3 Part 2) adds personality, religion, racial origin, culture and language, employment, standard of living, experience of caring for children, and skills. The Fostering Services National Minimum Standards (2011) expect that people receive information after their initial enquiry and are kept informed of any later application.

What this means for forms:

- Date of birth, household, accommodation, previous applications, relationships and criminal record are Stage 1 topics. Stage 1 only starts after an application.
- Most public primary web forms leave these out. That is consistent with the framework.
- The secondary "initial enquiry/referral" and "initial visit" forms described in procedures collect several of these topics before the application that Ofsted counts. These include household numbers, bedroom, health, relationships and working hours. In practice they work as an informal pre-Stage 1 with no statutory timescale.
- Independent agency forms more often ask Stage 1 topics at first contact: spare room, previous adoption and, on one form, criminal convictions.
- Ethnicity and religion are Stage 2 items. No public fostering primary form recorded asks them, but one hub's privacy notice says they are gathered at the first phone call.

**Criminal records.** Fostering-service work with contact with children is excepted from the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 under the 1975 Exceptions Order. OUCM's reading of the Order is that this applies to foster carers; the Order does not name "foster parent" explicitly. Regulation 26 makes anyone convicted of or cautioned for a "specified offence" committed as an adult unsuitable. Asking about it is lawful. The open question is *when*. Under UK GDPR Article 5(1)(c) (data minimisation) and Article 10, with the Data Protection Act 2018 Schedule 1 conditions for criminal offence data, collecting conviction data at first contact needs a documented condition and necessity. A conviction question on a first-contact form is difficult to justify when Stage 1 includes an enhanced DBS check anyway. (ICO guidance has not yet been re-read for this report.)

**Adoption.** The Adoption Agencies Regulations 2005, Part 4 as substituted in 2013, start Stage 1 at a formal registration of interest (regulation 21). The agency then agrees a Stage 1 plan, obtains enhanced DBS checks for the adopter and adult household members, and obtains medical reports and references. It must decide within two months, and the adopter has six months to continue to Stage 2. First4Adoption says there is no standard registration of interest form in England. Adoption England's 2025 front-door guidance for practitioners says the initial enquiry is not the time to settle questions about suitability; that name and contact details are what is needed; and that any monitoring questions should be explained and optional. It also says enquirers should be told the basic eligibility rules (age, residency and criminal history) and the agency's process, and invited to an information event before registering interest. So adoption has a named, regulated secondary form, the registration of interest, and national practice guidance on first contact. Fostering has neither, which is one reason fostering secondary forms vary so much in name and timing.

**Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland.** Welsh council notices cite the Local Authority Fostering Services (Wales) Regulations 2018 and its code of practice as the basis for enquiry processing. Scotland's mygov.scot tells people the assessment can take 6 to 12 months and covers medical history, criminal record and finances. The Looked After Children (Scotland) Regulations 2009, the Foster Placement (Children) Regulations (Northern Ireland) 1996 and current Department of Health NI guidance have not yet been re-read. Stage mapping for Scotland and Northern Ireland is therefore provisional.

## Mapping to the OUCM Enquiry schema v0.1

| Commonly asked item | OUCM field | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| How heard | `source` | Needs a controlled vocabulary. The observed option sets overlap heavily (see `answer_options.csv`). |
| Type of fostering or scheme | `fostering_interests` | Controlled list: short-term, long-term, short breaks, parent and child, supported lodgings, unaccompanied young people, disabled children's short breaks, emergency, adoption (out of scope flag), unsure. |
| Know more, ready to apply, transfer | `intent_tier` | A direct match to several forms' "how can we help" question. |
| Best days and times | `preferred_contact_window` | Direct match; free-text answers need mapping. |
| Postcode | `postcode_outward` + `la_gss_code` | Store the outward code only. Derive the council from it; this replaces "which council do you pay council tax to". |
| Web form, phone, event, social media advert form | `channel` | Direct. |
| Privacy acknowledgement | `lawful_basis` | Most councils rely on public task. Record the basis, not the tick. |
| Newsletter opt-in | `marketing_consent` | Record true only for a separate, unticked opt-in. A bundled sign-up should be recorded as not consented. |
| Response promise | `acknowledged_at`, `first_contact_attempt_at`, `first_contact_made_at` | These allow promises (one or two working days) to be measured without personal data. |
| Name, email, phone, address, previous names, partner details | – | Keep out; the schema already excludes direct identifiers. |
| Date of birth, health, convictions, previous adoption, spare room, employment, ethnicity, other residents | – | Keep out of first contact. These are Stage 1 or Stage 2 topics and belong, if anywhere, in a later Application slice. |
| Child named in an appeal | – | Keep out (children's data). |

`taxonomy.csv` carries this mapping for every topic in the dataset.

**Sketch of a later Application slice (descriptive only; no scoring, ranking or prediction).** It would hold `application_received_at`, consistent with Ofsted's application count; `stage` (pre-application visit, Stage 1, Stage 2, panel) with start and end dates; `checks_consented_at` for each check, recorded as dates only and never as results; `household_adult_count` and `bedroom_available` as yes or no, both only after application; `transfer_from_sector`; and `withdrawal_reason_code` using Ofsted's withdrawal categories. In 2024–25 these categories were led by change in household circumstances (36%), other or unknown (35%) and process not as expected (20%). It would not hold health, criminal record details, ethnicity or assessment content.

## Good practice (named examples)

- **A concrete response promise on a public page.** Foster for Greater Manchester and Foster Birmingham say someone will contact you within one working day. Foster Wales Bridgend adds that it aims to complete the initial visit and pre-assessment screening within five working days. PACT says it will call or email within three to five working days.
- **A staged timeline.** HSC NI says a social worker from the local team calls within two days and meets you at home within ten days.
- **"This is not an application".** Oldham's per-council page says submitting the enquiry is not a formal application and explains what happens to the data, including retention. Foster for East Midlands says you make no commitment by enquiring.
- **Separate consents.** HSC NI asks separately for contact consent and processing consent, linked to its privacy policy. Its newsletter is a separate sign-up. Adoption Matters offers an optional, unticked mailing-list box and says it will not add you without permission.
- **Plain enquiry-stage privacy notices.** Foster Wales notices from Newport, Caerphilly and Torfaen list enquiry-stage data and say it is not shared with other organisations at that point. Rhondda Cynon Taf states a 12-month retention after closure. Cardiff and Bridgend name the council as data controller and give the lawful basis at the form.
- **Explaining a question beside it.** One independent agency template explains the spare-room and residency rules next to the questions and tells enquirers that experience is not required. Compass explains why it asks for a postcode.
- **Choice of language.** Foster Wales council forms offer English or Welsh.

## Problems as patterns (counts, no services named)

Current counts are in [`../summary.md`](../summary.md). Patterns seen so far:

- Most public front doors give no concrete first-contact time.
- One public form signs people up to a newsletter automatically on submission. Several independent agency forms make a yes or no answer on marketing mandatory before an enquiry can be sent.
- Legacy enquiry pages stay indexed for boroughs that have moved to a new hub, and one consortium form routes enquiries for a council now in a different hub.
- Conflicting public details: phone hours that differ between two pages of one partner council; hub membership stated as 17, 18 and 20 councils across partner pages; a response promise of one working day on third-party guides but two on the hub's own page; differing contact email addresses for one hub; an enquiry form called an "online application form".
- No form offers save-and-return. That matters little for a short primary form, but more for secondary forms, which are not published.
- Two adoption agency forms require ethnicity at first contact, without saying it is for monitoring or can be left blank.

## Recommendations for the OUCM repository

1. Adopt Ofsted's "actionable enquiry, counted per household" definition by reference, and the DfE's milestone names (enquiry, expression of interest, initial visit, application start, panel, approval) as stage codes.
2. Publish `source` and `fostering_interests` vocabularies built from the observed option sets, with an `other` escape.
3. Keep the Enquiry slice free of date of birth, household, housing, health and conviction data. The evidence shows public primary forms already work without them.
4. Add a guidance checklist record per front door (response time, privacy link, separate marketing opt-in, what happens next, not-an-application statement, phone alternative). This assesses pages, never people or staff.
5. Next census pass: the hub forms that load in the browser, the Black Country, South East London and South West London hubs, large non-hub councils, the remaining Scottish councils, and adoption agencies in all four nations. Use per-council pages and published procedure manuals to recover forms that need JavaScript, and a person with a browser for the rest.

## Caveats

- Several hub forms did not render for automated readers. Their guidance was captured; their field lists were not.
- Required flags come from asterisks or "required" text in the rendered page. Where those were not visible, required is recorded as `unknown`.
- Some forms come from search-index snapshots, not live reads, and may have changed.
- Secondary-form content is secondhand throughout. No blank fostering application form was found published.
- "7,365 newly approved foster carers" counts carers, not households. Ofsted reports 4,430 mainstream households joining, 3,050 of them new to fostering.
