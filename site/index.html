Everyone who offers to care for a child deserves a quick, kind and considered reply.

Kinderform is a free, open project to make the first step of fostering and adoption kinder. It gives councils, hubs and agencies shared tools to reply well to the people who enquire.

Early days. Everything here is a draft, shared for comment.

Interest is not the problem

In England in 2024–25 there were almost 150,000 enquiries about fostering, and 7,365 newly approved foster carers. Some people enquire more than once, and services count enquiries in different ways, so the gap is not exact. But the government's own plan for fostering says plainly that interest is not the problem. People who offer to help can wait weeks or months for a reply.

Nobody designs it that way. Recruitment teams are small and stretched, systems differ from place to place, and there is no shared way to see how long people wait. That part can be fixed, and it is the part Kinderform works on.

Source: Department for Education, Renewing Fostering, February 2026, page 14. Checked 14 November 2026.

What we are making

An open standard for enquiries

Draft 0.1

A shared description of what an enquiry record holds, so any council, hub or agency can see how quickly people hear back. It holds no names and nothing about children, and it works with whatever form or case system a service already uses.

A picture of fostering front doors

Method in draft

A register of where people enquire across the UK, and a record of what each form asks and promises. It looks at pages and processes from the side of the person enquiring. Each service sees its own results first.

The evidence

Growing

A living library of research on how people come to foster and adopt, why some stop along the way, and why carers stay.

How we work

  • About adults who offer to care. Never children's records, and never decisions about children.
  • No real personal data. Every example is made up.
  • Nothing scores people. No tool here ranks, screens or predicts anyone who enquires.
  • Pages and processes, not staff. Recruitment teams are small and stretched, and they are not the problem.
  • Method first, then findings. Each service sees its own results privately, with time to reply, before anything is named.
  • Free and open. Everything is openly licensed, so anyone can use it without asking.

Who is behind this

Kinderform was started by Jamie Gannaway, co-founder of Deployed, which builds intake and routing systems for large organisations. This project gives that experience to the fostering and adoption sector for free.

It is not a Deployed product, it is not commercial, and it will never be a sales channel.
