# Front-door rubric (v0.1 draft for comment)

A way of looking at a fostering front door from the point of view of someone enquiring for the first time. It asks one question: does this page make a quick, kind and considered reply likely?

**This is a draft for consultation. It has not been used to score any named service, and it will not be until recruitment teams, carers and sector bodies have had the chance to change it.**

## How scoring works

Each criterion is scored 0 to 3:

- 0 absent, or likely to put people off
- 1 present but weak
- 2 adequate
- 3 an example others could copy

Scores are scaled to the domain weight. Weights add up to 100.

## Domains

| # | Domain | Weight | What is looked at | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Findability and routing | 10 | Form within two clicks of the council's fostering page; council page names the hub clearly; old or duplicate forms retired; postcode or authority routing | Page review |
| 2 | Ease of enquiring | 15 | Six or fewer required fields; phone or email, not both, required; no postal address unless something is posted; works on a phone; takes about two minutes | Page review, phone walk-through |
| 3 | Data minimisation and consent | 15 | No health, criminal-record or other sensitive questions at first contact; no date of birth; marketing opt-in separate and unticked; privacy notice linked at the form and names who holds the data | Page and privacy-notice review |
| 4 | Accessibility | 10 | Accessibility statement; labels and errors pass automated checks; plain English; language options; a non-digital route | Automated checks, page review |
| 5 | Setting expectations | 15 | A concrete response time; who will be in touch; an outline of the journey and how long it usually takes; confirmation on screen and by message | Page review |
| 6 | Responsiveness | 15 | Published service standard; reported time to first contact; share contacted within one working day; out-of-hours cover | Data the service publishes or provides |
| 7 | Other ways to get in touch | 5 | Phone with stated hours; callback slot; chat or text; bookable information sessions; chance to speak to a carer | Page review |
| 8 | Inclusion and openness | 5 | Clear answers on renting, being single, LGBTQ+, working, disability and faith; allowances published; a range of carer stories | Page review |
| 9 | Data quality | 10 | Captures arrival time, source, intent, fostering interest, routed authority and consent; one written definition of an enquiry; hand-off to the authority is logged | Service's data specification |

## Things that need fixing first

Any one of these caps the total at 50, because each can stop a suitable person at the door or breach their trust:

- questions about criminal convictions or health at first contact
- marketing consent made a condition of enquiring
- conflicting phone numbers for the same front door
- an old form still live alongside the current one

## How results will be handled

1. The method is published and open to comment before use.
2. Each service receives its own results privately, with time to correct errors and fix issues.
3. Public reporting starts with patterns across the sector and named examples of good practice.
4. Named results for every service are published only after steps 1 to 3, and always as an assessment of the page and process, never of staff.

## What the rubric cannot see

Domain 6 cannot be judged from a web page. A promise is not performance. Response-time data should come from services themselves, and no test enquiries should be submitted without a service's agreement, because they take up scarce staff time.

## Questions for reviewers

- Are the weights right? Should responsiveness count for more?
- Which criteria are unfair to small teams or to services tied to a corporate web platform?
- What is missing from the point of view of someone enquiring?
