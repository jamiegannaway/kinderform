# Adversarial audit · 26 November 2026

## Scope and status

The audit reviewed [main at a455638](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/tree/a455638be4384c5c13d9cccf20762b2e076a0219). It covered the mission, roadmap, research claims, census, analytical code, enquiry schemas, starter kits and public journeys. Repairs started from [e32eafb](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/commit/e32eafb64e59d2c16d66e62b8300042a18ec0eba), preserving the later mobile-chart and feedback changes. The branch then incorporated concurrent main commits through [c1e2c8d](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/commit/c1e2c8d), preserving the new research reports and register entries.

This is an AI-assisted source audit, with local reproductions and limited public HTTP checks. It is not independent human verification of the research, legal approval or proof of improved service outcomes. The repairs below are source changes in the audit-remediation pull request. Merge, deployment and live acceptance remain separate events.

No test enquiry was sent to a service. No real applicant records were processed. No new crawl, outreach, hosted intake or applicant scoring was introduced.

## Main judgment

Kinderform's mission is a quick, kind and considered reply to everyone who offers to care for a child. Its portable, service-owned tools and adult-side boundaries support that mission.

International coverage and presentation have advanced faster than independent checking and operational proof. A shorter form cannot repair an unowned inbox, failed delivery, unaccepted handoff or missing cover. The next useful milestone is a reviewed, bounded service implementation.

The audit also reproduced defects in analytical consistency, claim-review labels, validation and the boundary around named service scores.

## Findings and disposition

### AF-01 · Prove the receiving workflow

**Finding:** the adoption path led to a form kit, while ownership, cover, accepted handoff and overdue action were not a concrete deliverable. The monitoring record also required self-declared intent that the submission form did not collect.

**Repair:** the starter guide now has a first-reply worksheet and synthetic rehearsal. The Build page links the guide and worked enquiry. Intent can be absent until the enquirer declares it; it must never be inferred. The worked enquiry distinguishes acknowledgement, first human attempt and first two-way conversation.

**Still open:** stakeholder sessions, a receiving-team rehearsal and an agreed service-owned pilot. No improvement in real replies is claimed.

Sources: [initial roadmap](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/blob/a455638be4384c5c13d9cccf20762b2e076a0219/docs/roadmap.md#L131), [updated worked enquiry](../one-enquiry.md), [starter guide](../../starter/guide_templates/guide.md), [schema guide](../../schema/README.md).

### AF-02 · Assessment content is not automatically an earliest-collection rule

**Finding:** the advice inferred when a question may be asked from topics appearing in statutory assessment stages. It told services to move later topics to assessment without establishing an explicit legal restriction on earlier questions.

**Repair:** public wording separates the assessment map from Kinderform's design proposal. It explains that required assessment content does not itself ban an earlier question. Timing needs review by a legal or practice lead. Draft interpretation is marked locally.

**Still open:** qualified review of the relevant provisions, amendments, scope and timing. This repair does not decide that any particular question is lawful or unlawful.

Sources: [original inference](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/blob/a455638be4384c5c13d9cccf20762b2e076a0219/site/src/lib/insights.ts#L143), [advice](../../site/src/components/for-services.tsx), [law sample](../checks/law-sample-england.csv).

### AF-03 · Make checks specific to the claim revision

**Finding:** the withdrawal finding had new, explicitly unreviewed figures but retained a green source-check badge dated before the addition. Unchecked sources had no local label. Some headlines also overstated causal evidence or search completeness.

**Repair:** edited findings lose stale checks; draft extractions and findings needing review are labelled. Remaining recorded checks are bound to their unchanged finding text and source URL by a digest. Data validation and the site build reject an edited claim carrying an old digest. Universal trial-search and causal headlines are qualified.

The digest prevents accidental reuse of a check. It is not evidence that a person reviewed a claim, and this repair does not record any new human check.

**Still open:** checking unreviewed claims against originals and independently reviewing interpretations. At audit baseline, the sample worksheets had 0/60 topic rows and 0/92 England law rows checked. These figures describe those worksheets, not every review anyone may have undertaken elsewhere.

Sources: [original mixed check](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/blob/a455638be4384c5c13d9cccf20762b2e076a0219/evidence/findings.json#L62), [current findings](../../evidence/findings.json), [review guard](../../tools/evidence_review.py).

### AF-04 · Use one analytical definition and a stated unit

**Finding:** the same 57 UK forms produced 7 and 9 sensitive-form counts in different research components. A question about the ages of children someone could foster was tagged as sensitive applicant age. Repeated Australian form URLs received extra weight through regional register entries.

Reproductions at the audit baseline:

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| UK sensitive forms, two research components | 7/57 versus 9/57 |
| Correct only the child-age classification | Australian sensitive forms 9/30 → 8/30 |
| Deduplicate exact Australian URLs only | 30 → 27 forms; sensitive forms 9 → 7; median questions 7 → 6 |

The last two rows are separate counterfactuals, not one combined estimate.

**Repair:** shared question-level sensitivity and counting helpers feed the analytical views. Proven topic collisions are corrected. Form statistics count a URL once per country and stage, ignoring fragments and a trailing slash while preserving query parameters. Service-location rows and source dates remain in the census. Counts of locations and forms are separate.

**Still open:** independent review of complete forms and a wider classification sample. URL deduplication is an explicit statistical choice. Distinct URLs can share a template, and one URL can change over time.

Sources: [original UK predicate](https://github.com/jamiegannaway/kinderform/blob/a455638be4384c5c13d9cccf20762b2e076a0219/site/src/lib/forms-differ.ts#L93), [shared helpers](../../site/src/lib/census.ts), [semantic regressions](../../site/scripts/check-census-metrics.mjs).

### AF-05 · Preserve uncertainty about branches and absent features

**Finding:** all 1,388 primary question rows from the automated reader had blank conditions. Explicit follow-ups were counted as always-asked questions. Blank conditions did not establish that a question was unconditional. An automated failure to find a privacy notice also did not establish its absence.

**Repair:** `branching_status` distinguishes conditional, unconditional and unknown. Known follow-ups are excluded consistently; unknown branches remain in recorded-question counts and are disclosed. The converter recognises explicit follow-up labels. Unsupported automated privacy-notice negatives are changed to unknown. Original observation dates are preserved rather than implying a new visit.

**Still open:** browser review of branches, completeness and negative claims. These source repairs are not a replacement for that review.

Sources: [census column guide](../../census/README.md), [converter](../../tools/crawl_to_census.py), [classification regressions](../../tests/validate_census_semantics.py).

### AF-06 · Exclude named scores from publishable builds

**Finding:** the old gate excluded named scores only when `VERCEL_ENV=production`. The ordinary build generated `/form-check/named`. A preview label and `noindex` do not authenticate a reader. The public production route returned 404 during the audit; present production exposure was not observed.

**Repair:** named results require an explicit local-development opt-in. Every static build excludes the route and public link, including preview and production builds with the local flag set. Artifact checks enforce this boundary.

**Still open:** consultation and each service's right of reply before any future named publication. Hosting privacy must never be inferred from the word preview.

Sources: [build gate](../../site/src/lib/build-env.ts), [artifact check](../../site/scripts/check-publication.mjs), [CI matrix](../../.github/workflows/checks.yml).

### AF-07 · A valid monitoring record is not anonymous

**Finding:** the monitoring schema implied records could be shared without identifying someone. Free-text fields could still contain identifiers; precise times, service and geography could remain linkable.

**Repair:** schema and guide now say records are potentially identifiable and remain private. Source codes should be controlled; public or cross-service export needs a separate review and aggregation process. Schema validity does not establish disclosure safety.

**Still open:** a service's lawful handling, retention, access and export arrangements. No real-data export was performed or approved.

Sources: [monitoring schema](../../schema/enquiry.schema.json), [schema guide](../../schema/README.md).

### AF-08 · Validate successful contact and event order

**Finding:** punctuation-only phone numbers passed validation. Conversation times before receipt passed even with format checking. Canonical and starter checks disagreed about unused contact details.

**Repair:** contact validation requires digits and rejects unused route fields. The monitoring schema requires the time for a claimed contact attempt. A semantic validator checks time-zone-aware event ordering. Valid and invalid synthetic examples cover these cases alongside browser/offline validation parity.

**Still open:** contact reachability cannot be proved by formatting checks. The semantic validator must accompany structural validation in a service implementation.

Sources: [submission schema](../../schema/enquiry-submission.schema.json), [monitoring validator](../../schema/validate_enquiry.py), [synthetic examples](../../schema/examples/).

### AF-09 · Test reassurance and complete tasks

**Finding:** “nothing you ask counts against you” was an absolute promise a service might not be able to keep. Example deadlines could contradict selected call days. Automated page checks did not establish branched-form task success.

**Repair:** the example forms use bounded ask-without-committing wording. Call-day examples respect the chosen days. Deadlines remain fictional examples, not a service calendar or guarantee. The local guide asks the receiving team to agree holidays, cover and an achievable promise.

**Still open:** practitioner, enquirer, native-language, keyboard and screen-reader review. The minimum field set remains a design hypothesis. Both interactive examples still need JavaScript; no new no-JavaScript form is claimed.

Sources: [reference form engine](../../site/src/components/reference-form/engine.tsx), [outstanding user tests](../roadmap.md).

### AF-10 · Bound international and impact claims

**Finding:** the country comparison was a highly unequal convenience sample. One country had one readable form. Pooled counts gave accidental weight to whatever was easiest to collect. Monthly before/after comparisons could confuse campaigns, staffing or seasonality with an effect.

**Repair:** headline charts show sample sizes and counts for small samples. Wording describes the forms read, not national prevalence. The evaluation guide treats before/after comparison as exploratory and calls for arrival cohorts, missingness and balancing measures.

**Still open:** coverage, sampling and external validity. Keep useful adoption, clear next steps, freedom to stop and staff workload alongside speed. Do not treat approval counts as the sole objective.

Sources: [country presentation](../../site/src/components/countries.tsx), [service ideas](../../site/src/components/for-services.tsx), [worked evaluation guide](../one-enquiry.md).

## Verification

The remediation checks are recorded with the pull request and its exact commit. Required local checks are:

```sh
for t in tests/*.py; do python3 "$t" || exit 1; done
npm test --prefix site
npm run build --prefix site
VERCEL_ENV=preview KINDERFORM_PRIVATE_NAMED_RESULTS=true npm run build --prefix site
VERCEL_ENV=production KINDERFORM_PRIVATE_NAMED_RESULTS=true npm run build --prefix site
python3 tools/plain_check.py site/dist
git diff --check
```

The last two build cases must contain no named-score artifact or public link. CI also builds with a development `NODE_ENV` to ensure it cannot enable local review. The analytical regressions invoke the actual TypeScript functions. Validation covers the new synthetic contact, event-order, branching and review-state cases. No passing test is recorded as a human source check.

Local remediation checks passed: all 14 Python scripts, TypeScript checking, analytical regressions and 27 compiled browser-validator cases. Offline route checks passed in all 12 locales. Default, preview, production and development-`NODE_ENV` static builds excluded named scores. A loopback-only development server with explicit opt-in served the local review route; it was then stopped. The combined branch was rechecked after incorporating the concurrent main changes.

The repaired census currently counts 189 unique form URLs from 192 location entries, with 33/189 asking a recorded sensitive question. UK views agree on 9/57. Australia is 6/27 after the combined corrections. Unknown branching remains disclosed for 1,483 counted questions. These figures describe this source revision, not independently verified national practice.

The draft.4 starter candidate freezes the revised rules and operating guide. Earlier archives retain their original bytes. A candidate archive is not a GitHub release or a production deployment.
